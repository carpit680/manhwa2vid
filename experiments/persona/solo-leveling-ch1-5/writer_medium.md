Sung Jin-Woo bleeds out on a stone floor. A massive blade takes his arm. Stone giants tower over him as he wonders how it came to this.

In Seoul, Jin-Woo walks to a raid site. He is an E-rank hunter, widely known as the "World's Weakest." He barely scrapes by, fighting not for glory, but to pay his sick mother's hospital bills. The work is dangerous, and he is always getting hurt. Even normal people look down on him. 

At the site, he grabs a coffee from a food truck and chats with the vendor. The other hunters, older men like Mr. Kim and Bak, gossip about him. They joke that his presence means the dungeon will be weak. Jin-Woo hears them, but smiles it off. When he tries to get a coffee for himself, the vendor tells him they are out. 

Miss Ju-Hee, a B-rank healer, spots him. She scolds him for being hurt again, asking how he managed to get injured in an E-rank dungeon. He admits he was the only one hurt, and that the higher-ranked hunters didn't even bother bringing a healer. She sighs, exasperated but clearly fond of him. 

Mr. Song, an older, higher-ranked hunter, calls for attention. Since he is the strongest, the group agrees he should lead the raid. They enter the D-rank gate. Inside, they battle goblins. Ju-Hee heals Jin-Woo, who took a hit despite the dungeon's low rank. He feels embarrassed, holding a single, low-value E-rank magical core. He thinks about how little it will fetch. 

Suddenly, someone shouts. They have found a second entrance within the dungeon—a Double Lair. Mr. Song inspects the tunnel. It is dark and unmapped. Normally, they would report this to the guild and wait for orders, but Song points out that if other hunters clear it, their profits will vanish. He suggests they take a vote. 

The group is split. Eight vote to enter, eight vote to leave. It comes down to Jin-Woo. He looks at his single core. He thinks of his sister's college tuition and his mother's hospital bills. He needs the money. He votes to go in.

The group presses into the unmapped tunnel. Forty minutes of walking brings them out of the dark and up to a set of massive, ornate double doors. While they march, Jin-Woo quietly apologizes to Ju-Hee for swinging the vote. She is absolutely furious. She reminds him that a few inches higher and that goblin would have pierced his heart, to say nothing of his arms and legs that she just barely stitched back together. She demands to know if he hurt his head too, yelling that she is the only reason he is still breathing. Then, she softens. She tells him that if he is really that sorry, he can treat her to a meal. He blushes, stammering an agreement, but before they can figure out the details of this makeshift date, the tunnel ends.

A couple of the older hunters hesitate at the towering doors, asking if this looks a little too dangerous. But Mr. Song shakes his head, refusing to return empty-handed after coming all this way. He tells anyone who is scared to leave, declaring he will go in alone if he must. The rest of the party trusts his offensive power. If you are keeping count, having a C-rank veteran leading an expedition into a D-rank gate is usually a guaranteed safety net. So, they agree to follow him to the bitter end. The giant doors push open much easier than anyone expects.

They walk into a massive, domed sanctuary bathed in the blue glow of perimeter torches. It looks completely abandoned. There are no monsters waiting to ambush them, and no boss in the center of the room. Instead, they find a giant magic seal carved into the stone floor and dozens of towering statues lining the circular walls. The stone figures are carved as heavily armored guards. Most of them hold massive weapons, though a few carry musical instruments. 

Looming at the far end of the temple is the biggest statue of them all. It is a monolithic figure seated on a stone throne, wearing a spiked crown and a blank, unsettling stare. Jin-Woo shivers, feeling like something is watching them, but the others brush off his nerves. They scatter to inspect the room, amazed by the sheer scale of the ruins.

Someone spots a stone tablet held by a smaller, winged statue and calls Mr. Song over to look. The text is written in an ancient runic script, which Song is surprisingly able to translate aloud. It lists the Carthenon Temple's commandments. First, worship the Lord. Second, praise the Lord. Third, prove your faith. As Song reads the final line, which warns that those who break the commandments will never return alive, Ju-Hee tugs frantically on Jin-Woo's sleeve. She stammers that the giant statue's eyes just moved. She swears it was looking right at them.

Jin-Woo tells her she must be mistaken, but the air in the room suddenly drops to freezing. I think the artwork here does a fantastic job shifting the mood from curious exploration to sheer terror, dropping out all the background noise and leaving only a chilling, heavy silence. Suddenly, the massive entrance doors slam shut behind them with a deafening crash.

One of the younger hunters loses his nerve entirely. He yells that this was a huge mistake, tells the rest of the group they can keep the boss and the treasure, and marches back to the exit. He grabs the heavy iron handle, pulling at the door. The stone guard standing right next to him moves. It swings a massive, rusted halberd downward. The hunter's upper half vanishes in a geyser of red blood, leaving only his legs to collapse to the floor. The statue slowly pulls its weapon back and returns to a resting pose, exactly as it was.

Ju-Hee screams, burying her face in Jin-Woo's jacket. Panic erupts across the room as the hunters realize the statues are alive. That dead man was a D-rank hunter. The realization hits Jin-Woo like a freight train. If a single, casual strike from a peripheral guard statue can obliterate a D-rank combatant, this is absolutely not a D-rank dungeon. He stares past the screaming men, looking up at the giant on the throne. Its stone eyes are now glowing with a furious, blinding light.

Jin-Woo knows exactly what it means to be hopelessly outmatched. I should explain that normally, this profession works on a simple economic loop: hunters harvest magic cores from weak monsters, sell them to buy better weapons and armor, and use that gear to fight stronger monsters for richer payouts. But Jin-Woo is stuck at the absolute bottom of that ladder. He risks his life in weak dungeons just to pay his mother's hospital bills, fighting with cheap blades that shatter on goblin skin. He has broken away from his party and gotten lost. He has nearly starved to death trapped in a cave. He has spent weeks in a hospital bed after taking a single hit from an E-rank beast. He is the World's Weakest, a joke to the men around him, and he knows exactly what terminal danger feels like.

That instinct is screaming at him right now. The colossal statue on the throne leans forward, its glowing eyes flaring brilliant white. Jin-Woo grabs Ju-Hee by the shoulders and throws himself toward the stone floor. He screams for everyone to duck just as searing beams of energy erupt from the giant's eyes, vaporizing the space where the hunters were just standing.

The god's eyes erupt. A blinding column of pure, white-hot destruction obliterates the temple floor. Jin-Woo tackles Ju-Hee into the stone, his screamed warning the only reason she is not vaporized instantly. Dust clears to reveal an absolute slaughter. Half the raid party is just gone. Searing scorch marks replace the spaces where men were standing a second ago. Mr. Kim, an older hunter with thick sideburns, stammers that they would all be wiped out if Jin-Woo had not yelled. The air smells heavily of ozone and charred meat. Jin-Woo forces himself up, ears ringing, and sees the true, horrific cost of his deciding vote.

Mr. Song is on his knees, clutching a ragged shoulder where his right arm used to be. The veteran hunter grimaces, his face pale from the sudden shock. He tells Jin-Woo that he will be fine, and just asks him to help stop the bleeding. Jin-Woo rips a piece of his own jacket and ties a frantic tourniquet around the older man's shoulder. He asks Song what rank this monstrosity actually looks like. Song admits he has only experienced a B-rank raid once or twice, but this thing is leagues beyond that. He whispers that it is an A-rank, or maybe even an S-rank threat. I think the art conveys this sheer hopelessness perfectly, framing the colossal statue from low, distorted angles so it dominates the entire ceiling. It looks exactly like a towering, indifferent deity staring down at ants.

The narrative abruptly flashes back ten years to establish the broader stakes of this world. The sky tears open over a modern city, birthing the massive Gate that connects the other dimension to ours. This is the moment humanity awakened as hunters. I should explain that in this universe, an awakened person's power level is completely static from the very day they get it. If you awaken as an E-rank, no amount of intense training, gym time, or magical artifacts will ever boost you to a D-rank. It is a rigid, unforgiving caste system. Jin-Woo has spent his entire career stuck at the absolute bottom. He fights monsters he has no business facing just to keep his sick mother alive. Now, he is trapped in an S-rank slaughterhouse with a fragile, E-rank body.

Back in the temple, the surviving hunters are completely paralyzed by fear. Ju-Hee is catatonic, trembling so hard she can barely breathe, let alone cast a healing spell on Song's missing limb. Mr. Song sighs, looking at his shattered party. He admits he only brought three healers today because he thought this would be a quick, easy run. Now, one healer is a smear of ash on the floor, and another is curled into a tight ball against the wall, weeping and begging for his life. Song whispers that they need to stay perfectly still. If they move, the giant shoots. Should anyone sprint for the massive doors, the stone guards cleave them in half. It is like a sick, deadly game of red-light-green-light where losing a single step means immediate, violent disintegration.

One of the younger hunters finally snaps. Refusing to die trapped like a rat in an unmapped tomb, he declares he just signed a lucrative contract with a major guild and has his whole life ahead of him. The kid boasts about his incredible agility. "If anything, I'm confident in my own speed," he shouts, telling the others he will slip through the exit doors in a split second. He bolts. Sprinting across the stone tiles, he pushes his legs to their absolute limit. But the colossal statue's gaze tracks him effortlessly. A searing flash of golden light illuminates the room. The hunter is instantly reduced to nothing but vapor. Only his scorched sneakers bounce across the floor, smoking quietly on the tile.

Jin-Woo stares at the smoldering shoes. His mind races to make sense of the carnage. He realizes these monsters are not acting like normal dungeon beasts. They do not just blindly massacre hunters on sight. If it wanted them all dead, the giant could wipe them out in a single blink. It would be easier than squashing a bug. So why isn't it? Jin-Woo pieces the grim mechanics together. The peripheral statues attack only when someone approaches the door. The throne statue fires energy beams only when someone moves from their spot. This is exactly like a raid boss puzzle in an MMO where ignoring the underlying gimmick guarantees a total party wipe. It is an execution, but it is an execution with a very specific set of rules.

A frantic spark of hope lights up Jin-Woo's chest. He yells to Mr. Song, desperately asking what the first rule was on that stone tablet. Song winces through his agonizing pain. He recalls the Carthenon Temple's commandments, stating the first is to worship the Lord. Jin-Woo looks up at the massive, terrifying figure on the throne. Its eyes are glowing again, charging up another lethal barrage to wipe out anyone who twitches. "Mr. Song... the Lord..." Jin-Woo points at the monolithic giant. "Means that one, right?" He realizes they are not in a standard combat dungeon at all. They are trapped inside a twisted religious ritual, and the only way out is to prove their faith by playing along with the god's insane rules.
