Sung Jin-Woo bleeds out on a cold stone floor. Massive armored statues loom over him with raised weapons, their stone eyes glowing in the dark. He pants heavily, clutching a severed arm. He is E-rank, the absolute bottom of the barrel, and he admits he never expected to die like this. But to understand how the world's weakest hunter ends up butchered by giant stone knights, we have to rewind.

Jin-Woo arrives at a dusty construction site in Seoul. He is a scruffy guy in a hoodie who fights monsters just to pay his sick mother's hospital bills. He walks past heavy machinery and heads for the staging area. He greets a worker and asks for a coffee. Meanwhile, Mr. Kim, an older hunter, spots a buddy named Bak. Kim asks Bak what he is doing back in the game. Bak laughs and explains his wife is pregnant with their second son, so he needs the cash. Kim agrees that there is nothing like a raid to earn a fortune. 

Then Kim spots Jin-Woo and calls out to him. He asks if Jin-Woo has eaten yet. Jin-Woo smiles and thanks Mr. Kim for worrying about him. Nearby, a couple of other hunters watch the exchange. One asks if Jin-Woo is some kind of powerful veteran since everyone is so happy to see him. The other hunter snorts. He tells the rookie that Jin-Woo is actually known as the World's Weakest. He explains that Jin-Woo is so fragile he manages to get injured even in E-rank dungeons, which is basically the tutorial level of monster hunting. The rookie realizes this means their current D-rank dungeon will be a breeze, since the system scales to accommodate the weakest link. The older hunter shushes him, warning that Jin-Woo might hear them. Jin-Woo definitely hears them. He sighs, noting that it feels bad to be the punchline. 

To make matters worse, the vendor tells Jin-Woo that they just ran out of coffee. Jin-Woo mutters that this just is not his day. Then Miss Ju-Hee marches up to him. She is a B-rank healer, and she demands to know why his face is busted up again. Jin-Woo rubs his neck and explains he got hurt while hunting. Ju-Hee asks what actually happened to land him in the hospital. He admits he was the only one hurt in an E-rank dungeon. He tells her the rest of his party was so high-ranked they didn't even bother bringing a healer just because they assumed they would be safe. Ju-Hee is furious. She asks how they could be so careless. Jin-Woo just smiles and says he is used to it. 

Mr. Song, an older, high-ranking hunter, steps up to the crowd. He announces that he would like to be the party leader for this raid, and he asks if everyone is alright with that. Kim says they have no problems since Song is the highest rank there. The others quickly agree. So Mr. Song tells everyone to head into the gate. Kim pats Jin-Woo on the back and tells him to try not to get injured behind them. Jin-Woo awkwardly agrees. 

Inside the dungeon, the raid goes exactly as expected. The veteran hunters tear through the beasts like wet paper. Kim slices a beast in half, while Mr. Song burns another to ash. It is a slaughter. But true to his reputation, Jin-Woo still manages to get slashed across the leg. Ju-Hee kneels beside him, using her glowing magic to close the wound. She scolds him again, asking why he is so adamant about working as a hunter when it brings so many risks. She tells him she is not saying this for an apology, she is just deeply worried. Jin-Woo thanks her. He thinks about telling her he does it for the money, but he decides that would just be too embarrassing. 

The battle winds down. Kim laughs and says the raid is a piece of cake. Another hunter pulls a glowing crystal from a monster carcass. He explains this is a magical core. A core from a C-rank monster is worth thousands, which is a fortune for these guys. But Jin-Woo looks down at his own hand. He only managed to secure a single E-rank core. After all his injuries and all his trouble, he is walking away with pocket change. He admits to himself that considering he puts his life on the line, the payout is just too small. 

Suddenly, a hunter yells out from the back of the cavern. He tells everyone there is another entrance. The party gathers around a dark, ominous tunnel carved into the rock. Mr. Song peers into the gloom and notes that the rumors of a Double Lair are actually true. He stares at the swirling energy inside the tunnel. He explains that since the gate is still open, the real boss must be hiding down there. Usually, protocol dictates they contact the guild and await official orders. But Mr. Song warns them that if other hunters get here first, their profits will dwindle to nothing. So he proposes a risky alternative. 

He tells the group he wishes for all of them to go kill the boss. But since it could be dangerous, he asks the seventeen hunters to take a vote. Majority rules, and no complaining after the results. If you are keeping count, this is exactly the kind of greed that gets side characters killed. The hunters start calling out their choices. Kim votes to fight. Another hunter says no. The votes tally up quickly, splitting the party right down the middle. It ties at eight to eight. 

Everyone turns to Jin-Woo. The entire decision rests on his shoulders, which is probably smart when you're the weakest in the room. Jin-Woo grips his worthless magic core. He thinks about his lack of funds. He thinks about how he needs to send his little sister to college. Most of all, he thinks about his sick mother lying unconscious in a hospital bed. He needs the money, no matter the risk. Jin-Woo looks at the dark tunnel, his eyes hardening, and says, "I'm going!"

The party trudges through the dark cavern for forty long minutes. One hunter complains about the distance, asking exactly how long they have to walk. Mr. Kim tells him they have about twenty minutes left before the gate closes. Jin-Woo falls into step beside Ju-Hee. He awkwardly apologizes for dragging her down here after she voted no. She turns to him with an absolutely terrifying glare. She tells him she is definitely not okay. She asks if he is completely insane. She yells that a slightly higher stab wound would have put a hole straight through his heart. She reminds him that his arms and legs were basically shredded. Jin-Woo shrinks back. He admits he only survives because he owes her so many heals. She asks if he is actually sorry. He quickly replies that he is. So she tells him to treat her to some food. Jin-Woo blushes. He stammers like an idiot. She demands to know if he hates eating with her. He loudly denies it. Then someone at the front shouts that they finally found the boss room.

They stand before a set of towering, intricate double doors. A hunter mutters that he has never seen a room with doors like this before. Mr. Kim asks if this place seems too dangerous. Mr. Song agrees that it does. But he asks the group if they really want to return empty-handed after coming this far. He steps up to the massive doors and pushes. They swing open with surprising ease. Blue magical flames ignite in massive braziers along the walls. The lights reveal a colossal, domed cavern. It is the Carthenon Temple. Mr. Song tells everyone to scatter and scout the area. Dozens of towering stone knights line the walls, covered in moss and holding massive weapons. Some are even holding stone instruments. Jin-Woo shivers. He mutters that it feels like someone is staring at them. A hunter tells him to stop being so negative. At the far end of the room sits an absolutely gargantuan statue on a stone throne. It looks like a forgotten god. A hunter asks if that is the boss. Another hunter replies that it is just a statue. He notes that there are zero monsters in the room.

Then a hunter spots a glowing magic circle on the floor. He yells for Mr. Song, explaining that there is something written on a smaller winged statue. Mr. Song walks over and studies the runic text. He explains that these are the Carthenon Temple's Commandments. First, worship the Lord. Second, praise the Lord. Third, prove your faith. Suddenly, Ju-Hee violently grabs Jin-Woo's sleeve. She is trembling. She tells him the huge statue's eyeball just moved. She explains that it was looking right at them. Jin-Woo tells her she must be mistaken. But then a freezing chill washes over the room. Mr. Song reads the final line on the tablet. "Those who do not follow these commandments, will never return alive." Before anyone can even process the threat, a deafening crash echoes through the chamber. The massive doors slam shut.

Two armored statues now block the exit. A hunter completely loses his nerve. He yells that this is all nonsense. He tells the group that coming here was a massive mistake. He announces that he is leaving. He marches straight for the door, telling the others they can keep the treasure. He reaches for the heavy iron handle. Mr. Song screams at him to stop. It is too late. The statue swings its massive stone sword in a blinding blur of motion. The blade effortlessly decapitates the arrogant hunter. Blood paints the door and splatters across the floor. The statue smoothly returns to its resting position. The rest of the party screams in pure terror. Mr. Kim yells that the statues are alive. Ju-Hee buries her face in Jin-Woo's chest, sobbing hysterically.

Jin-Woo stares in absolute horror. He asks himself what rank that dead hunter was. He realizes the man was D-rank, which makes him infinitely stronger than Jin-Woo. Yet the statue killed him in a single, effortless hit. Jin-Woo knows this makes no sense. He knows a D-rank dungeon should never have monsters this strong. If the door guards can move, he realizes the colossal statue on the throne can probably move too. Jin-Woo knows the bitter taste of death better than anyone in this room. He remembers his pathetic career. He recalls getting lost in his first raid and nearly dying. He thinks about getting hospitalized for weeks by a lowly E-rank goblin. He even remembers almost starving to death while trapped in a low-level dungeon. While other hunters trade magical cores for better weapons, Jin-Woo fights in a plain hoodie with a cheap blade. He endures the sneering remarks from his peers. He knows they call him the World's Weakest. He knows exactly how fragile a human body is when a monster hits it.

Jin-Woo looks back up at the colossal statue. The giant stone face is staring right down at them. The statue's massive eyes begin to glow with a blinding, terrifying light. The sheer magical pressure is suffocating. If you have ever ignored a raid mechanic in a video game, you know exactly what is about to happen. Jin-Woo's instincts scream at him to move. He grabs Ju-Hee and throws his body toward the stone floor. He screams at the top of his lungs, "EVERYONE, DUCK!!!" A devastating beam of pure energy erupts from the statue's glowing eyes, engulfing the entire chamber in blinding white fire.

The blinding white fire clears, leaving behind the suffocating stench of scorched stone and vaporized blood. Jin-Woo presses his face into the freezing floor. He coughs up thick dust, his ears ringing from the sheer concussive force of the blast. Beside him, Mr. Song groans in absolute agony. The older hunter clutches a ragged stump where his right arm used to be. Blood pours onto the cracked tiles like a spilled drink. Jin-Woo realizes they only survived because he yelled. If they had hesitated for a single second, they would be ash right now.

A surviving hunter tries to lift his head from the rubble. But Mr. Song violently screams at him, telling everyone to remain perfectly still. He explains that if anyone stands up, the colossal statue will absolutely attack them again. Jin-Woo stares at the veteran hunter, then asks if Mr. Song knew the energy beam was coming. Song admits he only shouted because his instincts screamed at him to drop. It was pure, primal terror speaking. Jin-Woo crawls slightly closer to the injured man. He asks Mr. Song what rank this dungeon actually is. Song grimaces, his face pale from massive blood loss, and tells Jin-Woo that he has never seen anything above a D-rank. But he admits this magical pressure easily exceeds a B-rank raid. He explains that this nightmare might even be an S-rank anomaly.

This is exactly the kind of situation every hunter dreads. Ten years ago, the mysterious gates first tore open the sky, connecting the human world to a dimension of brutal monsters. Random people awakened with magical abilities to fight back against the threat. But there is a cruel, unbreakable catch. Your power level never changes from the day you awaken. If you start as an E-rank weakling like Jin-Woo, you stay an E-rank weakling forever. You just have to survive the grind. And right now, survival looks completely impossible.

Ju-Hee trembles violently in the dark corner. She stares blankly at the charred remains of her former party members. The trauma is completely breaking her mind. Song asks her for some basic healing, then tells her his bleeding is not stopping. He asks if she can just focus on his arm. But she is too paralyzed by fear to even move her glowing hands. She just sobs uncontrollably into her knees.

Then the suffocating pressure finally breaks someone. A younger hunter pushes himself up from the floor, telling the group that this situation is completely insane. He admits he absolutely refuses to die in a low-level dungeon like this. He tells them he just signed a contract with a major guild, then explains that his agility stat is incredibly high. So he tells everyone he will just use his speed to bypass the door guards. He drops into a sprinter's stance and launches himself forward, moving like a blurred shadow. He actually makes it halfway to the massive doors in a single heartbeat. 

But the Carthenon Temple does not care about your impressive stats. As the runner nears the exit, one of the towering guard statues snaps into motion. It does not even bother to draw its massive stone weapon. It simply steps forward and brings a colossal stone foot down on the fleeing hunter. The sickening impact sounds like a bursting melon. The statue crushes him instantly, leaving only a smear of blood on the stone floor. 

The remaining hunters watch in pure, unfiltered horror. Another man punches the ground in despair. He asks the others why the monsters are playing with them, explaining that these statues could wipe them out in a single second. He asks why they are just waiting. 

Jin-Woo watches the fresh blood pool around the guard statue's foot. His mind races through the chaos. He tells himself the panicked hunter is completely right. The statues are infinitely stronger than them, so squashing them would be easier than stepping on a bug. He asks himself why the giant god on the throne is not just spamming its laser beam. Then it finally clicks. Jin-Woo realizes this room operates like a twisted puzzle. Regular monsters blindly attack hunters on sight. But these statues only attack under very specific conditions. The door guard only swings when someone approaches the exit. The giant statue only fires when someone moves incorrectly. 

Jin-Woo looks over at the stone tablet holding the Carthenon Temple's rules. He remembers the text Mr. Song read earlier, so he asks Mr. Song to repeat the very first commandment. Song winces through his pain. He replies that the first rule is to worship the Lord. Jin-Woo stares at the colossal statue sitting on the throne. He tells Song that the giant stone monster is obviously the Lord. He explains that this entire room is a brutal test of faith. 

If you want to survive a boss mechanic, you have to play by the developer's rules. Jin-Woo looks closely at the giant, glowing eyes of the statue. He notes that the massive monster only burns people who stay on their feet. He tells himself that bowing is the ultimate form of worship. So Jin-Woo makes a terrifying, desperate gamble. 

He slowly begins to push himself up from the floor. Mr. Song stares at him in utter disbelief. He asks Jin-Woo what he is doing, then tells the boy he is going to get himself killed for no reason. But Jin-Woo does not stop. He ignores the panicked warnings and rises to his knees, then his feet. He locks eyes with the colossal statue, his expression dead serious, and says, "I won't die like this." He tells the older hunter that he finally understands the pattern. The giant statue's eyes flare with blinding golden light, charging up the energy to incinerate him on the spot.
