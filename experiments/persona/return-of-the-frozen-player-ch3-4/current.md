The glowing blue hologram floating in front of Jun-Ho displays a brutal reality check, showing his original abilities drastically reduced. His magic stat sits at a pathetic eighteen. But as he scrolls through the text, a new entry catches his eye, and he asks himself if he can actually break the seal holding his friends. The screen lists his rank as a five, explaining that he is the one who defeated the floor's master. It also gifts him something unprecedented: Frost (EX). Deok-gu always said S-grade was the absolute ceiling, making our guy the very first to break that limit. The system tells him that his stats will increase by thirty every single time he clears a floor on the dimensional elevator. If he climbs the tower, he realizes he can reclaim his original strength. He tells himself he can make the queen of ice's power his own. Looking back at the frozen statues of the Nest Attack Team, he promises them he will melt the ice. "I'll get you out of there!"

Back in the sunny hospital room, Deok-gu nearly drops his jaw. The bald Player Association president paces the floor, asking why Jun-Ho wants to be a player again after spending his past life begging to retire. Jun-Ho casually slurps his soup, replying that twenty-five years have passed and he literally does not know how to do anything else. Deok-gu warns him it is a terrible idea, explaining the rise of fiends. He tells Jun-Ho that players who use their awakened powers to commit crimes are running rampant, acting like tumors in society. Since there are no CCTVs on the second floor and no country has a means to contain them anymore, he explains the fiends are having a field day. Deok-gu says that these criminals absolutely despise Specter and will go crazy trying to kill him the second he steps out of line. So he urges his old friend to just forget the hero business. When Jun-Ho refuses, Deok-gu suggests a compromise, telling him to throw the Specter name away and start over simply as Seo Jun-Ho.

Jun-Ho agrees to the name change but tells Deok-gu he still needs to reach the tenth floor. Deok-gu sighs, asking how he expects to survive the lava zone on the third floor without his old gear. Jun-Ho calmly sets his spoon down and admits he ate the core. Deok-gu freezes, his face twisting into a mask of pure horror as he screams that Jun-Ho ate the nucleus. Jun-Ho defends himself, explaining that as soon as he defeated the monster, the energy forced itself into his body. Deok-gu clutches the back of his neck, complaining about his blood pressure, which is entirely fair when your best friend treats a legendary boss drop like a mid-day snack. Jun-Ho shrugs off the panic, revealing he now possesses the frost skill and asks if Deok-gu thinks he can handle the heat now. Defeated, Deok-gu says he will cover for him, promising to silence the doctors who saw his unmasked face. Since his stats are sealed at level one, Jun-Ho asks the president to just print him a new player license.

But the world has changed while he was on ice. Deok-gu projects a digital document, explaining that the big guilds have grown more powerful than the Association itself. He says they made it a strict custom to attend the licensing exams, meaning he cannot just hand out a free pass under the table anymore. He tells Jun-Ho he has to take the public exam just like every other rookie. Jun-Ho complains that it sounds annoying, but quickly changes his tune. He tells Deok-gu that if he wants full support, he needs a good pretext to get it. He plans to score the absolute best results so that every major guild will beg to recruit him. Imagine being the examiner who has to grade a reincarnated legend.

The Player Association license exam site buzzes with nervous energy. Jun-Ho stands in the crowded lobby, wearing a black jacket and holding a ticket marking him as applicant number seventy-five. Finding an empty seat, he activates a system skill called Watchguard of Darkness, which enhances his hearing. The shadows around him flare purple as he listens in on the other test-takers gossiping across the room. They whisper to each other, saying that President Shim Deok-Gu is personally acting as the examiner today. Jun-Ho watches the holographic monitors as applicant number one steps into the testing arena. The guy wields a spear, trying to keep a mob of simulated monsters at bay. It works for a few seconds until the monsters close the distance, rendering the long weapon useless. The system flashes a fail message. Jun-Ho leans back, telling himself he understands the progression rhythm of the test while the examiners ruthlessly cycle through dozens of applicants who cannot even meet the basic requirements.

Finally, they call number seventy-five. Jun-Ho walks into the stark white arena carrying an absolutely ridiculous amount of gear. Up in the observation booth, the guild scouts stare at him in disbelief. One scout tells the room that the skinny kid brought a sword, a bow, throwing knives, a dagger, and even a spear. A blonde woman scoffs, calling him just another Specter copycat. Deok-gu sits quietly at the edge of the panel, sweating bullets because he specifically told Jun-Ho to stand out, but this walking armory routine is pushing it. Jun-Ho just smiles, stepping up to the starting line.

The system announces the start of the exam, spawning twenty holographic dwarves that look and act exactly like real goblins. The little green monsters rush him with rusted daggers. Jun-Ho readies his spear, noting that the simulation feels incredibly real. He swings the polearm in a massive arc, shattering the first wave into blue pixels. Then the system spawns more behind him. So Jun-Ho drops the spear and draws his bow, channeling the memory of Gilbert Green, the famous marksman who taught him archery. He fires multiple arrows in rapid succession, pinning the ambushers to the floor. When the final goblin lunges at him from the wall, Jun-Ho smoothly transitions to his sword. He executes a flawless, spinning slash that completely obliterates the monster in a flash of golden light. The system chimes, declaring that applicant seventy-five has passed. Jun-Ho drops his shoulders, panting heavily and complaining aloud that his unconditioned body is already in pain.

Up in the booth, the scouts are stunned. The dreadlocked scout admits his eyes deceived him, praising the kid's extraordinary weapon proficiency and clean swordsmanship. He tells the others he cannot believe a frail-looking guy pulled off such fluid combat. But then the blonde scout pulls up Jun-Ho's digital profile. She explains the screen lists his main skill simply as Weapon Proficiency at a measly D-rank. The scouts immediately lose interest. They agree that a D-rank main skill means his potential is severely capped, telling themselves he is a terrible investment for any top-tier guild. They decide to pass on him to avoid a loss. Sitting nearby, Deok-gu takes a slow, very satisfied sip of his coffee. He mentally apologizes for fabricating the profile, knowing perfectly well that by making Jun-Ho look like a low-ceiling prodigy, the Association can scoop him up without a single guild fighting them for him. The blue system card stamps a massive pink pass over Seo Jun-Ho's name, officially bringing the frozen player back into the game.
