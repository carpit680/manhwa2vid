Jun-Ho stares at the glowing blue interface floating in the dark. The system lists his new title: The One Who Opens Spring, and a new skill: Frost (EX). If you remember the lore drop from Deok-gu earlier, S-grade is supposed to be the absolute ceiling for abilities. EX breaks the scale entirely. The screen explains he earned this by killing the Frost Queen. It grants him a massive stat boost every time he ascends a floor on the dimensional elevator, plus a ridiculous five-hundred percent buff to stamina and magic regeneration. He reads the text, realizing he can make the queen of ice's power his own. Then, he can melt the ice and save the other four heroes. He tells himself to just wait a bit, promising he will get them out of there.

We jump back to the hospital room, picking up right where the last chapter left off. Jun-Ho casually eats a bowl of beef bone soup while wearing a wig, calmly telling Deok-gu that he wants to be a player again. The bald association president nearly has a heart attack. He reminds Jun-Ho that twenty-five years ago, he constantly complained about wanting to retire. Deok-gu asks why he would want to return now, suggesting he just rest up. But his real concern is the Fiends. I should explain this dynamic: Fiends are awakened players who use their abilities for murder and terrorism. They are basically society's tumors, running rampant on the tower's second floor because there are no surveillance cameras to track them. Deok-gu explains that no country has the means to contain them anymore, so they must be having a field day. If these criminals find out Specter is back, they will go crazy trying to kill him for revenge. So, Deok-gu tells him to throw the Specter name away and climb the tower as plain old Seo Jun-Ho.

Jun-Ho agrees, explaining he needs to reach the tenth floor to save their friends. Deok-gu asks how he plans to bypass the third floor's lava zone, which requires the Frost Queen's nucleus to survive. Jun-Ho pauses his meal. He casually admits he ate the core. Deok-gu grabs him by the collar, screaming about his blood pressure. Honestly, the story rushes this reveal a bit, treating a legendary item consumption like a punchline, but it gets us to the next plot beat. Jun-Ho defends himself, explaining the core forced itself into his body when he defeated her. He asks what else he was supposed to do, noting the upside is he now possesses the frost skill. Deok-gu asks if he can handle the third floor with it. Jun-Ho replies that he thinks so. Deok-gu sighs, agreeing to deal with the doctors who saw Jun-Ho's face. Since Jun-Ho's abilities and stats are sealed back to level one, Deok-gu promises to help him start over.

Jun-Ho asks for a new player license. Deok-gu explains that the big guilds now make it a custom to attend the licensing exams to scout talent, meaning he cannot just hand over a badge. Jun-Ho has to take the public test like everyone else. Jun-Ho smirks, deciding to put on a show so everyone will want him. He heads to the massive Player Association building, holding ticket number seventy-five. While waiting in the crowded lobby, he decides to see what the competition is talking about. He activates his Watchguard of Darkness skill. Dark purple energy swirls around his chair as he enhances his hearing to eavesdrop on the recruiters upstairs. The exam is a combat trial against holographic monsters. The first applicant steps into the grid-lined arena with a spear. Up in the booth, the recruiters note his fighting spirit is strong. He keeps the monsters at bay initially. But when they close the distance, he panics, waving his weapon aimlessly, and fails instantly. Finally, they call applicant seventy-five. Jun-Ho walks in looking like a walking armory. He carries a spear, a bow, throwing knives, a dagger, and a sword. Up in the viewing booth, the recruiters groan. They assume he is just another skinny Specter copycat trying too hard to look cool, complaining that he brought way too much gear.

The system announces the start, spawning twenty holographic enemies. The interface labels them Dwarves, though they look exactly like green, pointy-eared goblins—a funny translation quirk you just have to roll with. Jun-Ho smiles, telling the monsters to come at him. He starts with the spear. He swings it in a massive, fluid arc that shatters the first wave into blue pixels. But as soon as they drop, more spawn. He drops the spear and draws his bow. As he fires pinpoint shots into the rushing mob, he remembers Gilbert Green, the famous marksman from his old team who taught him archery. When the enemies get too close for arrows, Jun-Ho smoothly transitions to his throwing knives. He sinks blades into their foreheads with effortless wrist flicks. The art here does a great job showing his economy of motion; he never wastes a step as he shifts between ranges, turning a messy brawl into a rhythmic dance.

Finally, only one monster remains. Jun-Ho draws his sword. He steps forward and unleashes a blinding, explosive slash that wipes the final goblin out and leaves a glowing trail across the arena floor. He dramatically grabs his shoulder, complaining aloud that his body hurts too much and he needs to build his stamina back up. The examiner announces over the speaker that he passed. Up in the viewing room, the guild recruiters are stunned. They admit his weapon proficiency is extraordinary, noting how precisely he handled every tool despite his frail-looking body.

But then they pull up his digital profile. The screen lists his only main skill as Weapon Proficiency, and it is only D-rank. In this world's power system, a D-rank main skill means a player has a hard ceiling on their potential, making them a bad long-term investment—like a group project member who peaks on day one and never learns a new skill. The recruiters quickly lose interest. They explain it would be a waste to train him, deciding it is best to avoid a loss and look elsewhere. They refuse to go down a difficult path for a low-tier recruit. In the back of the room, Deok-gu quietly sips his coffee with a smug smile. He tells himself he only feels bad for fabricating Jun-Ho's profile. By faking a low-rank skill, the President ensures the Association can recruit Jun-Ho safely, keeping him hidden in plain sight while the major guilds walk away empty-handed.
